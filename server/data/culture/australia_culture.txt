Culture Guide: Australia

Social Norms:
Australians are known for being relaxed, informal, and direct. The culture strongly values egalitarianism — treating everyone as an equal regardless of status, wealth, or title. Excessive formality or pulling rank is frowned upon. Call your lecturers by their first name unless they specifically ask otherwise.

"Tall poppy syndrome" is a real cultural phenomenon — publicly boasting about your achievements or acting superior to others is looked down upon. Australians respect people who are successful but humble about it.

Australians communicate directly and use a lot of dry humour, sarcasm, and self-deprecation. Teasing and "taking the mickey" out of friends is a sign of affection, not hostility. Do not take gentle ribbing personally — join in if you can.

"No worries" and "she'll be right" are quintessentially Australian phrases expressing that everything is fine and not to stress. The pace of life is generally more relaxed than in Europe or North America, though this varies by city.

Tipping:
Australia has a minimum wage system that includes hospitality workers, so tipping is not expected or obligatory. It is appreciated but never required.

- Restaurants: leaving 10% for excellent service is generous and appreciated, but not expected. Many Australians do not tip at all.
- Cafés and coffee shops: no tipping expected
- Taxis: rounding up is common but not required
- Bars: no tipping expected
- Food delivery: tip is optional on the app

Do not feel pressured to tip if the service was average. Tipping culture in Australia is entirely optional.

Transport:
Australian cities are large and spread out — public transport varies significantly by city.

Sydney has the best public transport: trains, buses, light rail, and ferries. Get an Opal card for contactless travel across all modes. Melbourne has an extensive tram network (free in the CBD), trains, and buses — use a Myki card. Brisbane uses the Go Card for buses, trains, and ferries. Perth and Adelaide have trains and buses but are more car-dependent.

Most universities are reachable by public transport. Student concession cards are available for domestic students — international students may or may not be eligible depending on the state; check with your state's transport authority.

Cycling is popular in some cities (Melbourne, Adelaide, Canberra). Always wear a helmet — it is legally required across all of Australia.

Weather:
Australia's seasons are opposite to the Northern Hemisphere — summer is December to February, winter is June to August. The climate varies enormously: tropical in the north (Queensland, Northern Territory), temperate in the south (Melbourne, Tasmania), dry in the centre. Sydney and Brisbane are warm year-round. Melbourne is famous for having "four seasons in one day." UV radiation is extremely high across Australia — always use SPF 50+ sunscreen, a hat, and UV-protective sunglasses outdoors, even on cloudy days.

Food:
Australian food culture is diverse and multicultural. Coffee culture is strong — flat whites, long blacks, and oat lattes are serious business. Vegemite on toast is a genuine staple. Meat pies, Tim Tams, and Anzac biscuits are iconic.

Major supermarkets: Woolworths and Coles (the two main chains). Budget option: Aldi (widely available). IGA for independent local supermarkets. Most supermarkets are open 7 days a week including Sundays.

SIM Cards:
Australia has good mobile coverage in cities and towns. In rural and remote areas, coverage can be patchy. Major carriers: Telstra (best rural coverage), Optus, Vodafone. Budget prepaid options: Boost Mobile (Telstra network), Amaysim (Optus network), Circles.Life. Prepaid SIMs are available at convenience stores, supermarkets, and carrier stores. A reasonable plan costs 30 to 50 AUD per month.

Banking:
Open an Australian bank account as soon as possible. Major banks: Commonwealth Bank (CBA), Westpac, ANZ, NAB. Most offer student or everyday accounts with no monthly fee. You will need your passport and a TFN (Tax File Number) — apply for a TFN at the ATO website after arrival. Without a TFN, tax is withheld at the highest rate from any income. Digital banks like Up and ING offer fee-free accounts and are popular with students.

Sun Safety:
Australia has one of the highest rates of skin cancer in the world due to high UV levels. The "Slip, Slop, Slap, Seek, Slide" campaign summarises sun safety: Slip on a shirt, Slop on SPF 50+ sunscreen, Slap on a hat, Seek shade, Slide on sunglasses. Take sun safety seriously regardless of your skin tone or your home country's climate.
