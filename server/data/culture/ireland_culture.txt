Culture Guide: Ireland

Social Norms:
Irish people are known for being warm, welcoming, talkative, and hospitable. Conversation flows easily with strangers — in shops, on public transport, in pubs, and on the street. Small talk about the weather, sport, or where you are from is completely normal and genuinely friendly, not a prelude to being sold something.

Irish humour is central to social life — dry wit, self-deprecation, and good-natured slagging (teasing friends affectionately) are how Irish people show warmth and acceptance. If people are teasing you gently, it usually means they like you. Take it in good spirit.

Modesty is valued. Like Australia, Ireland has a version of tall poppy syndrome — boasting or showing off is considered poor form. Understated confidence is respected more than overt self-promotion.

"Craic" (pronounced "crack") is an Irish word for fun, good times, lively conversation, and general enjoyment. "What's the craic?" means "What's happening?" or "How are things?" Saying a night out had "great craic" is the highest compliment.

Punctuality is relatively relaxed in social situations — arriving 10 to 15 minutes late to a social event is normal. For academic, professional, or formal occasions, punctuality is expected.

Tipping:
Tipping in Ireland is appreciated but not as rigidly expected as in the US.
- Restaurants: 10 to 15% for good sit-down service. Check the bill — some restaurants add a service charge automatically.
- Cafés: not expected, though a tip jar may be present
- Taxis: round up or add 1 to 2 EUR
- Pubs: tipping at the bar is not traditional in Ireland — buying the bartender a drink ("one for yourself") is more common if you want to show appreciation
- Hotels: 1 to 2 EUR for porters and housekeeping is appreciated but not required

Transport:
Dublin has a reasonable public transport network: DART (coastal rail), Luas (tram), Dublin Bus, and commuter rail. Use the Leap Card for discounted fares across all modes — buy one at any newsagent or Spar. The TFI Live app shows real-time bus arrivals. Outside Dublin, public transport is limited — buses (Bus Éireann) connect major towns but frequency is low.

Cycling is viable in Dublin and other cities. Bleeper Bikes and Dublinbikes (bike share) are available in Dublin. Traffic can be busy in the city centre. Most university campuses are walkable.

Weather:
Ireland has a mild, wet, and highly unpredictable climate. Rain can occur at any time of year. Summers are mild (15 to 20°C) and pleasant but never hot. Winters are mild (5 to 10°C) but grey and very rainy. The west coast (Galway, Clare, Kerry) receives significantly more rainfall than the east (Dublin). A good waterproof jacket is more useful than an umbrella — wind makes umbrellas impractical. Layers are essential year-round. Do not be fooled by a sunny morning — always carry a light rain layer.

Food:
Traditional Irish food includes Irish stew, colcannon (mashed potato with cabbage), soda bread, full Irish breakfast (eggs, bacon, sausages, black and white pudding, beans, toast, and grilled tomato), and boxty (potato pancake). Tayto crisps are a national institution.

Major supermarkets: Tesco, Dunnes Stores, SuperValu, Aldi, Lidl. Aldi and Lidl are significantly cheaper and excellent quality. Most supermarkets are open 7 days a week.

The pub is the central social institution in Irish culture — it is a place for conversation, music, sport, and community, not just drinking. Many students socialise in pubs whether or not they drink alcohol. Non-alcoholic drinks are always available and no one will pressure you to drink.

SIM Cards:
Buy a prepaid SIM at any newsagent, Spar, Tesco, or phone shop. Major carriers: Three Ireland, Eir, Vodafone Ireland. Budget options: 48 (Three network — excellent value), GoMo (Eir network). 48 offers unlimited data plans for around 12 to 15 EUR per month on direct debit — one of the best value SIM plans in Europe. You do not need to show ID to buy a prepaid SIM in Ireland. Register online with a home address.

Banking:
Opening a bank account in Ireland can be slow. Bank of Ireland and AIB are the main traditional banks — both offer student accounts. N26 (online) is easier to open immediately. Revolut is widely used by students as a day-to-day account while waiting for a traditional bank account. You will need a PPS number and proof of address for a full Irish bank account. Your university accommodation confirmation letter is accepted as proof of address.

Student Life:
Irish universities have strong student unions and a vibrant society and club culture. Joining clubs and societies (hiking, sports, cultural, drama, music) is one of the best ways to meet people and feel settled. Rag Week (student charity fundraising week) and other university events are important social calendars. International Student offices at all Irish universities run orientation and social events specifically for international students — attend these in your first week.
