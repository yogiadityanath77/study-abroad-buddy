Culture Guide: United Kingdom

Social Norms:
Queueing (standing in line) is a serious social norm in the UK. Always join the back of a queue and never push in — this causes genuine offence. Queue at bus stops, shops, ticket machines, and anywhere people are waiting.

The British communication style tends toward understatement and indirectness. "That's quite interesting" can mean something is bad. "Not bad" often means good. "I might join you" often means no. Over time you will learn to read between the lines. People are generally polite and reserved with strangers.

Saying "sorry" is extremely common — people apologise even when something is not their fault (if someone bumps into you, both parties say sorry). This is social convention, not an admission of guilt.

Please and thank you are used constantly. Always say "please" when asking for something and "thank you" when receiving it — in shops, on public transport, everywhere.

Personal space is important. Avoid standing too close to strangers. Do not touch people you do not know well.

Tipping:
Tipping in restaurants is standard — 10 to 15% is typical. Some restaurants add a "discretionary service charge" to the bill — you can ask for this to be removed if you are unhappy with the service. Tipping in pubs when ordering drinks at the bar is not expected, though you can offer to "buy the barman a drink." Tip taxi drivers by rounding up.

Transport:
London has the London Underground (the Tube), Overground, Elizabeth line, buses, and National Rail. Get an Oyster card or use a contactless bank card — both are cheaper than buying paper tickets. Outside London, most cities have buses and some have trams. Trains connect cities — book in advance for cheaper fares (National Rail, Avanti, LNER, etc.).

In most UK cities outside London, students cycle or use buses. University cities like Oxford, Cambridge, and Bristol have excellent cycling infrastructure.

Food:
Major supermarkets: Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose (premium), Lidl, Aldi (budget). Lidl and Aldi are most affordable for students. Most supermarkets are open on Sundays, though hours may be reduced.

University student unions often have subsidised cafes and bars. Many universities have food banks for students facing financial hardship — these are confidential and non-judgemental.

SIM Cards:
Buy a prepaid SIM at any supermarket, newsagent, or phone shop. Popular prepaid providers: giffgaff (on O2 network), SMARTY (on Three network, data rollover), Lebara (cheap international calls). You do not need to show ID to buy a prepaid SIM in the UK, making setup very easy. Register online with a UK address once you have one.

Weather:
The UK climate is mild but unpredictable and frequently rainy. Bring waterproof clothing. Layers are essential — weather can change several times in one day. Summers are warm but not hot. Winters are grey and damp rather than extremely cold.

Banking:
Opening a UK bank account requires proof of address and identity. Traditional banks (Barclays, HSBC, NatWest, Lloyds) are often reluctant to open accounts for new arrivals without a UK credit history. Easier options for international students: Monzo (online, quick setup), Starling Bank (online, fee-free), or student accounts at Barclays/HSBC with a student visa and university letter. You will need a UK address.

NHS Registration:
Register with a local GP (general practitioner / doctor) as soon as you arrive. Find your nearest GP at nhs.uk and register — this is free. You do not need to be unwell to register. Having a GP registered before you need one means you can access healthcare quickly when needed.
