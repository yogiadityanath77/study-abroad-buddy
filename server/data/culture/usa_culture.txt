Culture Guide: United States of America

Social Norms:
Americans are generally friendly, informal, and outgoing with strangers. It is normal for people you do not know to smile, say hello, or make small talk in lifts, shops, or on the street. This is social convention, not an invitation to a deep conversation — a simple smile and "Hi, how are you?" (answered with "Good, thanks, you?" regardless of how you actually feel) is the standard exchange.

First names are used almost universally — professors, supervisors, and colleagues will typically introduce themselves by their first name and expect you to use it. Using titles like "Professor" or "Dr." is appropriate until you are invited to use first names.

American communication style is direct but also positive and enthusiastic. Phrases like "That's a great idea" or "Awesome" are used frequently and do not necessarily carry the weight they might in other cultures. Criticism is often softened — feedback may be delivered as "That's good, but maybe consider..." rather than directly.

Tipping:
Tipping is not optional in the United States — it is an expected part of the payment system. Service industry workers (servers, bartenders, hairdressers, taxi drivers) earn below minimum wage on the assumption that tips will make up the difference.

Standard tipping rates:
- Restaurants (sit-down): 18 to 22% of the pre-tax bill. 15% is considered low. Many card machines will suggest 18%, 20%, and 25% automatically.
- Food delivery: 15 to 20%
- Taxi and rideshare (Uber, Lyft): 15 to 20%
- Bartenders: 1 to 2 USD per drink, or 15 to 20% for a tab
- Hotel housekeeping: 2 to 5 USD per night left on the pillow
- Coffee shops with tip jars: optional, 1 USD is typical if you feel it is warranted
- Fast food and counter service: not traditionally expected, though tip prompts now appear on card machines

Tipping is not expected at: fast food restaurants (traditional counter service), self-checkout, petrol stations.

Transport:
Public transport quality varies enormously by city. New York City has an extensive subway system. Chicago, Boston, Washington DC, San Francisco, and a few other cities have reasonable public transit. Most American cities are car-dependent and public transport is limited or unreliable.

For most university campuses: most universities have free or subsidised shuttle buses connecting campus buildings, student housing, and nearby areas. Cycling is possible on some campuses.

Rideshare apps (Uber, Lyft) are widely used and reliable in cities and suburbs. Consider whether you need a driving licence — in many non-urban areas, having a car makes life significantly easier.

Food:
American portion sizes are large — restaurant portions are often enough for two people. Doggy bags (taking leftovers home) are completely normal and expected. Ask your server for a "box" or "to-go container."

Grocery chains vary by region. National chains include Walmart, Kroger, Safeway, Whole Foods (premium), Trader Joe's (mid-range, good quality). For budget grocery shopping: Aldi, Lidl (in some states), WinCo, and Food4Less.

Tipping culture extends to food delivery apps (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub) — tip 15 to 20% on delivery orders.

SIM Cards:
Buy a prepaid SIM at any phone carrier store or Walmart/Target. Major carriers: AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile (best coverage in most areas). Budget MVNOs on the same networks: Mint Mobile (T-Mobile network), Visible (Verizon network), Cricket (AT&T network). Mint Mobile offers very competitive rates — around 15 USD per month for unlimited data if you pay 3 months at a time. You will need a US address and a US payment method or cash.

Sales Tax:
Prices displayed in shops and restaurants do not include sales tax. Tax is added at the register and varies by state and sometimes by city — typically 5 to 10%. Budget for this when shopping.

Healthcare:
The US healthcare system is private and expensive. Always carry your health insurance card. If you have a non-emergency medical issue, go to an Urgent Care clinic (cheaper and faster than an Emergency Room). Emergency rooms are for genuine emergencies only — an ER visit without insurance can cost thousands of dollars.

Banking:
Open a US bank account as soon as possible. You will need your passport, student visa (I-20), university ID, and a US address. Major banks with student accounts: Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citi. Avoid excessive ATM fees — use your bank's own ATMs. Venmo and Zelle are widely used for peer-to-peer payments between friends.
