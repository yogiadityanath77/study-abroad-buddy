Mental Health and Wellbeing for Students Abroad

Culture Shock:
Culture shock is a normal psychological response to living in a new cultural environment. It typically follows a pattern: initial excitement (honeymoon phase), followed by frustration and disorientation (crisis phase), gradual adjustment, and eventually adaptation. Most students experience some degree of culture shock in the first 3 to 6 months abroad.

Common symptoms: mood swings, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, homesickness, irritability, loss of appetite, feeling overwhelmed by small tasks, social withdrawal.

Coping strategies: maintain routines, stay connected with family and friends at home, join student clubs and societies, explore your new city gradually, be patient with yourself, seek support from your university's international student office.

University Mental Health Services:
Most universities in Germany, UK, and France offer free or low-cost counselling for enrolled students. Services are confidential. Contact your university's student services or international office to find out what is available. Examples:
- Germany (Studentenwerk): psychological counselling (Psychosoziale Beratung) is free for enrolled students at most universities
- UK: most universities have a student wellbeing centre with counsellors, mental health advisors, and crisis support
- France: university health services (Service de Santé Étudiante, SSE) offer psychological support

Loneliness and Social Isolation:
Loneliness is one of the most common challenges for international students. Strategies to build a social life: attend university orientation events, join student societies and sports clubs, use platforms like Meetup and Internations, take language exchange classes, volunteer locally.

Sleep and Exercise:
Maintaining good sleep habits and regular exercise significantly reduces anxiety and depression. Many universities have free or subsidised gym facilities for students.

Crisis Support:
If you or someone you know is experiencing a mental health crisis:
- Germany: Telefonseelsorge crisis helpline: 0800 111 0 111 (free, 24 hours)
- UK: Samaritans: 116 123 (free, 24 hours)
- France: Numéro National de Prévention du Suicide: 3114 (free, 24 hours)
- International Association for Suicide Prevention: https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/

Do not hesitate to contact emergency services (112 in Europe, 999 in the UK) if there is immediate danger.
