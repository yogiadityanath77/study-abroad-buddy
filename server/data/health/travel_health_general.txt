Travel Health for Students Going Abroad

Vaccinations and Immunisations:
Before travelling abroad for an extended period, students should review their vaccination status and consult a travel health clinic or GP at least 6 to 8 weeks before departure. Some vaccines require multiple doses over several weeks.

Routine vaccines to ensure are up to date:
- MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella) — two doses required
- Tdap (Tetanus, Diphtheria, Pertussis) — booster if more than 10 years since last dose
- Influenza (flu) — annual vaccine recommended
- COVID-19 — follow current guidance for your destination country
- Varicella (chickenpox) — if you have not had chickenpox or been vaccinated

Destination-specific vaccines commonly recommended for Europe:
- Hepatitis A — recommended for most travellers; two doses provide long-term protection
- Hepatitis B — recommended especially if you may receive medical or dental treatment abroad, or if you might have new sexual partners; three-dose series
- Meningococcal disease — some universities require proof of meningococcal vaccination before moving into student accommodation
- Rabies — generally not required for Europe but recommended if you plan extensive outdoor activities

Food and Water Safety in Europe:
Tap water is generally safe to drink throughout Western and Northern Europe (Germany, France, UK, Netherlands, Austria, Switzerland, Scandinavia). In some parts of Eastern Europe, bottled water is recommended. Always check local guidance on arrival.

Food hygiene standards in the EU are generally high. Standard food safety precautions: wash hands before eating, avoid raw or undercooked meat and seafood, check expiry dates on packaged food.

Health Insurance:
This is critically important for students going abroad. Options depend on destination:

Europe (EU/EEA countries):
- EU students studying within the EU can use their European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) for access to state healthcare at the same cost as local residents. Apply through your home country's national health authority.
- Non-EU students must purchase comprehensive private health insurance. Many European universities require proof of health insurance for enrollment.
- Germany: statutory health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, GKV) is compulsory for students under 30 enrolled at a German university. Major providers include TK (Techniker Krankenkasse), AOK, and Barmer. Cost is approximately 120 EUR per month.
- France: students under 28 are covered by the French social security system (Sécurité Sociale Étudiante) after registering. Complementary insurance (mutuelle étudiante) is recommended.

UK:
- Students on a UK Student Visa pay the Immigration Health Surcharge which gives access to the NHS. No additional insurance is required for healthcare in the UK, though dental and optical care are not fully covered.

Mental Health:
Living abroad can be stressful and isolating. Most universities offer free or subsidised counselling and mental health services for international students. Register with these services early — do not wait until a crisis. Common student experiences include homesickness, culture shock, academic pressure, and loneliness. These are normal and support is available.

Emergency Contacts (Europe):
- Emergency services (police, fire, ambulance): 112 (works across all EU countries and many non-EU European countries)
- Germany ambulance: 112, police: 110
- UK ambulance and emergency: 999 or 112
- France ambulance (SAMU): 15, police: 17, fire: 18, European emergency: 112

Prescription Medications:
Carry enough prescription medication for your entire stay plus extra in case of delays. Carry medications in original labelled containers. Bring a letter from your doctor listing your medications, dosages, and diagnosis. Some medications that are legal in your home country may be controlled or restricted in your destination country — check before travelling.

Dental and Eye Care:
Get a dental check-up and any outstanding dental work done before departure — dental treatment abroad can be expensive for non-residents. If you wear glasses or contact lenses, bring a copy of your prescription and an extra pair.
