Germany Student Visa (National Visa Type D)

Students from non-EU countries who want to study in Germany for more than 90 days must apply for a National Visa (Type D) for the purpose of studying. EU citizens do not need a visa to study in Germany.

Required Documents:
- Valid passport (must be valid for at least the duration of your stay)
- Completed visa application form (available at the German embassy website)
- Biometric passport photograph (35mm x 45mm, white background)
- University admission letter or enrollment confirmation from a recognised German university
- Proof of financial means: blocked account (Sperrkonto) with at least 11,904 EUR (as of 2024), or a scholarship letter, or a formal obligation letter from a sponsor
- Proof of health insurance valid in Germany (travel health insurance for the visa application; statutory health insurance required after arrival)
- Proof of accommodation in Germany (university dormitory confirmation, rental contract, or host family letter)
- Academic certificates and transcripts (secondary school leaving certificate, previous university degrees if applicable)
- Language proficiency certificate: German language proof (TestDaF, DSH, Goethe-Zertifikat) for German-taught programmes, or English proficiency (IELTS, TOEFL) for English-taught programmes
- Motivation letter or statement of purpose explaining why you chose the programme and university
- CV or resume (curriculum vitae)

Processing Time:
The processing time for a German student visa is typically 4 to 12 weeks. Apply at least 3 months before your intended travel date. The embassy may request additional documents which can extend processing time.

Application Process:
Apply at the German embassy or consulate in your home country. Book an appointment online through the embassy website. Bring original documents and copies. Pay the visa fee (approximately 75 EUR). After arrival in Germany, register at the local residents registration office (Einwohnermeldeamt) within 14 days and apply for a residence permit at the Foreigners Authority (Ausländerbehörde).

Blocked Account (Sperrkonto):
A blocked account is the most common way to prove financial means. You deposit the required amount (currently 11,904 EUR per year or 992 EUR per month) into a German blocked account before your visa is approved. Providers include Deutsche Bank, Fintiba, Expatrio, and Coracle. The funds are released monthly after you arrive in Germany.

After Arrival:
Register your address (Anmeldung) within 14 days at the local Einwohnermeldeamt. Apply for a residence permit at the Ausländerbehörde with your passport, visa, registration certificate, enrollment confirmation, and proof of health insurance. The residence permit is typically valid for the duration of your study programme plus 3 months.

Working Rights:
Student visa holders in Germany can work 120 full days or 240 half days per year without a work permit. Working more than this requires approval from the Federal Employment Agency.

Embassy Links:
