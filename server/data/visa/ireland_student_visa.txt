Ireland Student Visa

Non-EEA (European Economic Area) students who want to study in Ireland must apply for a Study Visa (also called a D Study Visa) for courses longer than 3 months. EEA and EU citizens do not need a visa.

Required Documents:
- Valid passport (valid for at least 12 months beyond your intended arrival date)
- Completed online visa application form (at visas.inis.ie)
- Visa application fee: 60 EUR for a single-entry visa, 100 EUR for a multi-entry visa
- Letter of acceptance from an Irish educational institution that is on the Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP)
- Proof of financial means: evidence that you can cover tuition fees and living costs. Ireland recommends showing at least 10,000 EUR available in addition to your paid tuition fees
- Proof of tuition fees paid or a scholarship letter
- Private medical insurance valid in Ireland for the duration of your stay
- Academic transcripts and previous educational certificates
- English language proficiency evidence if your course is taught in English
- Two recent passport photographs
- Evidence of accommodation in Ireland (offer letter from student accommodation, rental agreement, or host family letter)

Processing Time:
Apply online at visas.inis.ie at least 8 weeks before your intended travel date. Processing typically takes 6 to 8 weeks. Some nationalities may have longer processing times.

After Arrival — IRP Registration:
All non-EEA students must register with the Irish Naturalisation and Immigration Service (INIS) and obtain an Irish Residence Permit (IRP) card within 90 days of arrival. In Dublin, register at the Burgh Quay Registration Office — book an appointment online as slots fill up quickly. Outside Dublin, register at your local Garda (police) station. Registration fee is 300 EUR. Bring your passport, letter of acceptance, proof of address, and proof of private medical insurance.

Stamp 2:
After registration, you receive a Stamp 2 in your passport (or on your IRP card), which grants permission to remain in Ireland as a student. Stamp 2 allows you to work and study under specified conditions. You must renew your IRP annually for the duration of your studies.

Working Rights:
Stamp 2 holders can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and up to 40 hours per week during holiday periods (June to September and December to January). You do not need a separate work permit — Stamp 2 itself authorises this work. Register for a Personal Public Service (PPS) number to work legally — apply at your local Department of Social Protection Intreo office.

Health Insurance:
Ireland does not provide free public healthcare to international students (unlike the UK's NHS). Private medical insurance is compulsory for your visa application and IRP registration. Most universities offer or recommend insurance plans. Laya Healthcare, Irish Life Health, and VHI are major Irish health insurers.

Tuition Fees:
EU and EEA students pay the student contribution charge (approximately 3,000 EUR per year). Non-EEA students pay full international tuition fees, which typically range from 10,000 to 25,000 EUR per year depending on the university and programme.

Key Institutions:
Major universities include University College Dublin (UCD), Trinity College Dublin (TCD), University College Cork (UCC), NUI Galway (University of Galway), and Dublin City University (DCU). Dublin is the most expensive city — Cork and Galway offer lower living costs.
