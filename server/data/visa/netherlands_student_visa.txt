Netherlands Student Visa and Residence Permit

EU and EEA citizens do not need a visa or residence permit to study in the Netherlands. Non-EU students who want to study in the Netherlands for more than 90 days need a residence permit, and most will also need an entry visa (MVV).

Most-Visited Route (MVV + Residence Permit):
Most non-EU students apply through their Dutch university via the Most-Visited Route (MVV procedure). In this process, the university (as a recognised sponsor) applies for your entry visa (Machtiging tot Voorlopig Verblijf, MVV) and residence permit on your behalf. This means you do not apply at the Dutch embassy yourself in most cases — your university's international office handles the application with the IND (Immigration and Naturalisation Service).

Required Documents (submitted through your university):
- Valid passport (valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay)
- Passport photograph meeting Dutch requirements
- University acceptance letter / enrolment confirmation
- Proof of sufficient financial means: at least 1,141 EUR per month (as of 2024) for living costs, or the full amount for the duration of your stay
- Proof of health insurance valid in the Netherlands
- Proof of paid tuition fees or scholarship letter
- Antecedents certificate (certificate of good conduct) from your home country for some nationalities
- Academic transcripts and diplomas
- In some cases: TB (tuberculosis) test — required for students from certain countries

Process:
Your university submits the MVV application to the IND on your behalf. Processing typically takes 2 to 4 weeks. Once approved, you pick up your MVV sticker at the Dutch embassy or consulate in your home country. Travel to the Netherlands within the validity of the MVV. After arrival, your university arranges collection of your residence permit (verblijfsvergunning) — you do not go to the IND yourself in most cases.

After Arrival:
Register with the municipality (gemeente) at the local Basisregistratie Personen (BRP) within 5 days of arrival — your university will guide you through this. You will receive a BSN (Burger Service Nummer), which is the Dutch equivalent of a national ID number. You need your BSN for: opening a bank account, registering with a GP (huisarts), and paying taxes if you work.

Health Insurance:
As an international student in the Netherlands, you are not automatically covered by the Dutch national health insurance. Most students need to arrange their own health insurance. If you work more than 8 hours per week, you are legally required to take out Dutch statutory health insurance (zorgverzekering) with a registered Dutch insurer. If you do not work, private international health insurance is acceptable. Many universities partner with AON or other providers for student health insurance.

Working Rights:
Non-EU students with a residence permit can work up to 16 hours per week during term time and full-time during June, July, and August. Your employer must have a work permit (TWV) from the UWV on your behalf. Working more than permitted without authorisation is a violation of your residence permit conditions.

Tuition Fees:
EU students pay the statutory tuition fee (approximately 2,530 EUR per year in 2024–2025). Non-EU students pay the institutional tuition fee, which varies significantly by university and programme — typically 8,000 to 20,000 EUR per year for Bachelor's, and 10,000 to 25,000 EUR per year for Master's programmes.

Cities and Student Life:
Major student cities include Amsterdam, Delft, Leiden, Utrecht, Groningen, and Eindhoven. Amsterdam is the most expensive. Groningen and Eindhoven are more affordable. Most Dutch universities teach a large proportion of Master's programmes in English. Dutch people generally speak excellent English.
