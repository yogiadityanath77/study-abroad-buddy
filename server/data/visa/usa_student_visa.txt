USA Student Visa (F-1 Visa)

International students who want to study full-time at a US university, college, or academic institution must apply for an F-1 Student Visa. This is the most common student visa for the United States.

Required Documents:
- Valid passport (must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay)
- Form DS-160 (Online Nonimmigrant Visa Application) — completed and submitted online at ceac.state.gov
- Visa application fee payment receipt (MRV fee — currently 185 USD)
- SEVIS fee payment receipt (currently 350 USD for F-1 students) — paid at fmjfee.com before the visa interview
- Form I-20 (Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status) — issued by your US university after acceptance
- Proof of financial support: bank statements showing sufficient funds to cover tuition plus living costs for at least the first year. Tuition varies widely — budget at least 10,000 to 60,000 USD per year depending on the institution, plus 10,000 to 15,000 USD for living costs
- Academic transcripts and certificates
- Standardised test scores (SAT, ACT for undergraduate; GRE, GMAT for graduate; TOEFL or IELTS for English proficiency)
- Proof of ties to your home country (property, family, job offer) to demonstrate you intend to return after studies
- Passport photograph meeting US visa photo requirements

SEVIS:
SEVIS (Student and Exchange Visitor Information System) is a US government database that tracks international students. Your university will register you in SEVIS and issue your I-20. You must pay the SEVIS I-901 fee before your visa interview. Keep your I-20 safe — you will need it every time you enter the US.

Visa Interview:
You must attend an in-person interview at a US embassy or consulate in your home country. Schedule your appointment as early as possible — wait times at some embassies can be several months. During the interview, be prepared to explain your choice of university and programme, your financial support, and your plans after graduation. Demonstrate clearly that you intend to return home after completing your studies.

Processing Time:
Processing time varies significantly by country and time of year. Apply at least 3 to 5 months before your programme start date. Visa processing itself is typically 3 to 5 business days after the interview, but interview appointment wait times can be much longer.

After Arrival:
Your F-1 visa allows you to enter the US up to 30 days before your programme start date listed on your I-20. At the port of entry, a Customs and Border Protection officer will stamp your passport and I-94 record. Check your I-94 record online at cbp.dhs.gov within a few days of arrival. Register with your university's international student office (Designated School Official, DSO) within the first week. Maintain full-time enrolment status — dropping below full-time requires prior DSO approval.

Working Rights:
F-1 students may work on-campus up to 20 hours per week during term and full-time during official university breaks. Off-campus work requires authorisation: Curricular Practical Training (CPT) for work related to your degree during studies, or Optional Practical Training (OPT) for up to 12 months after graduation (STEM graduates may apply for a 24-month OPT extension).

Health Insurance:
The US does not have a national health system. Health insurance is essential and expensive. Most universities require students to have health insurance and offer a university-sponsored plan. Costs typically range from 1,500 to 3,000 USD per year. Review coverage carefully before waiving the university plan for a private one.

Social Security Number:
You cannot apply for a Social Security Number (SSN) until you have a job offer or are enrolled in a work programme (CPT/OPT). You do not need an SSN to open a bank account or study — do not let anyone pressure you into applying before you are eligible.
